Three ways to make a rock

Igneous rocks crystallize from molten rock. Cooling speed controls crystal size. Magma that cools slowly deep underground grows large crystals you can see, as in granite. Lava that cools quickly at the surface makes tiny crystals, as in basalt, or even glass, as in obsidian.

Sedimentary rocks form at the surface. Older rock is broken down by weathering, and the pieces are carried by water, wind or ice and laid down in layers. Over time the layers are buried, compacted and cemented into sandstone, shale or limestone. Because they form at ordinary temperatures, sedimentary rocks are where almost all fossils are found.

Metamorphic rocks are existing rocks transformed by heat and pressure without melting. Minerals recrystallize and often line up in bands called foliation. Shale becomes slate, then schist, then gneiss as conditions intensify. Limestone becomes marble.

A cycle with no beginning

In the 1780s the Scottish farmer and naturalist James Hutton realized that these processes connect. Mountains erode into sediment, sediment becomes rock, rock is buried and transformed or melted, and new rock is pushed back up to erode again.

Hutton concluded that Earth must be unimaginably old for this cycle to have run so many times. He wrote that he found "no vestige of a beginning, no prospect of an end." That idea, now called deep time, is the foundation of this course.

Field note

Siccar Point, Scotland

In 1788 Hutton took two friends by boat to this headland. There, nearly vertical layers of gray rock are capped by gently sloping red sandstone. The lower layers had to be deposited, buried, tilted on end, lifted up and worn flat before the upper layers were laid on top. Hutton's companion John Playfair later wrote that "the mind seemed to grow giddy by looking so far into the abyss of time."

Key terms

Intrusive
Igneous rock that cooled underground. Slow cooling gives large crystals.
Extrusive
Igneous rock that cooled at the surface. Fast cooling gives small crystals or glass.
Lithification
The process of compacting and cementing loose sediment into solid rock.
Clastic
Sedimentary rock built from fragments of other rocks, like sandstone.
Foliation
Parallel bands or sheets in a metamorphic rock, caused by minerals aligning under pressure.
Parent rock
The original rock that was transformed into a metamorphic rock.

Check your understanding

Try each question before you open the answer. These aren't graded.

Q1You find two igneous rocks. One has crystals you can see, and the other has crystals too small to see. Which one cooled underground?
The one with visible crystals. Slow cooling underground gives crystals time to grow large.
Q2Why are fossils almost never found in igneous rock?
Molten rock destroys organic remains. Fossils are preserved when organisms are buried in sediment at normal surface temperatures.
Q3Marble and limestone are both made mostly of the mineral calcite. What's the difference?
Marble is limestone that has been recrystallized by heat and pressure. Its crystals interlock, and the original grains, pores and fossils are erased.
