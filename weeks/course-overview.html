Welcome

Every cliff, riverbank and road cut is a record of the past. Geologists read those records the way historians read documents: layer by layer, looking for clues about what happened and when. By the end of this course you'll be able to do the same.

No background in science is needed. Each week builds on the one before, so work through them in order. The course is fully online, and you can do the work at any time of day as long as you meet the weekly deadlines.

How each week works

Every week follows the same five steps, in this order.

  1. ReadTwo short readings introduce the week's big ideas.
  2. StudyA diagram or chart shows the key idea at a glance.
  3. CheckUngraded questions let you test yourself before moving on.
  4. ActivityA lab, map or writing task. This is what you submit.
  5. ReflectA short field journal entry connects the week to your world.

Course policies

Late work

Activities submitted up to three days late lose 10%. After that, contact your instructor. If something serious comes up, reach out early: extensions are easier to arrange before a deadline than after.

Academic integrity

Your work should be your own. You're welcome to discuss ideas with classmates, but write your own answers and cite any sources you use.

Accessibility

If you have an accommodation plan, share it with your instructor in Week 1. Every diagram in the course has a text description, and every chart has a data table.

Communication

Course updates are posted as announcements. Check them at the start of each week. Email is the best way to reach your instructor.
